\chapter{Introduction}
\label{ch:introduction}

Small offices run on one ever-growing spreadsheet. A coaching institute's fee register is typical: one
row per student per month, with the student's name and phone, the batch, the batch teacher and the
teacher's phone, the month, the fee paid and the payment mode. Over a year it reaches thousands of rows
and develops the classic anomalies of an unnormalized relation \cite{codd1970,kent1983}:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Update anomaly.} A teacher's phone number is repeated on every row of every student of
  that teacher; when it changes, a few old rows are always missed, and the sheet now holds two numbers.
  \item \textbf{Inconsistency.} ``Riya Sharma'' and ``riya sharma'' are counted as two students, so totals
  and dues are wrong.
  \item \textbf{Insertion and deletion anomalies.} A new teacher cannot be recorded until a student pays;
  deleting the last row of a batch deletes the only record of who teaches it.
  \item \textbf{Query cost.} ``Who has not paid for October?'' takes an hour of filtering.
\end{itemize}

In database terms the sheet violates third normal form: \code{teacher\_phone} depends on
\code{teacher}, which depends on \code{batch}, which depends on the student, which is only part of the
key (\code{student\_name}, \code{month}). The people who suffer from this do not know the vocabulary,
and the tools they use do not help: database front-ends such as Airtable or NocoDB expect the user to
design the tables, and chat assistants answer one question about a file without leaving a correct
database behind.

\textbf{QLens} closes this gap. It reads the sheet into PostgreSQL, discovers from the data itself which
columns determine which (functional dependencies) and which columns identify a row (candidate keys),
shows the rows that break an almost-true rule as inconsistencies, finds near-duplicate spellings, and
then synthesizes a third-normal-form design \cite{bernstein1976}. With one click the design is created as
real tables with primary, unique and foreign keys, the data is migrated in a single transaction, and the
migration is accepted only if joining the new tables back gives exactly the original rows. The
objectives were therefore:

\begin{enumerate}[label=O\arabic*.]
  \item Do the analysis \emph{inside} the database, with SQL and PL/pgSQL, so that its correctness rests
  on the same engine that will hold the data.
  \item Produce designs that are provably in 3NF and lossless, and verify losslessness on the real rows.
  \item Isolate every user and workspace by PostgreSQL privileges, not by application convention.
  \item Present every result in plain language, for users who have never written SQL.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Scope of the Work}
\label{sec:scope}

\paragraph{In scope.} Import of one CSV or XLSX sheet per workspace (up to 20\,MB, 100{,}000 rows and
60 columns); SQL type inference and profiling; discovery of candidate keys of up to three columns and of
exact and approximate functional dependencies with a left-hand side of up to two columns; near-duplicate
detection and repair with an audit log; minimal cover and 3NF synthesis; generation of the DDL; a
transactional apply with a lossless check; a sandboxed, read-only query editor with history; and
three bundled sample sheets with planted errors (a coaching fee register, a kirana shop's sales and a
hostel mess register) so that the product can be demonstrated without private data. QLens keeps its own
records in a normalized metadata schema, which is itself a main subject of this report.

\paragraph{Out of scope.} Other database engines, multi-sheet workbooks, live collaboration, writes from
the query editor, normal forms beyond 3NF (BCNF can lose dependencies; 3NF synthesis cannot), and the
hardening needed for public multi-tenant hosting. QLens runs locally on one laptop against a private
PostgreSQL 18 cluster.

\paragraph{Status.} The report describes the system at the time of submission and marks each capability
as \stdone{} (implemented and covered by automated tests: 229 backend tests and 63 frontend tests pass),
\stwip{} (partly implemented) or \stplan{} (designed, not yet started). Nothing is described as working
unless it is marked \stdone.

\section{Product Scenarios}
\label{sec:scenarios}

\paragraph{The coaching-institute owner.} \stdone{} The owner imports the fee register (3{,}000 rows:
300 students, 8 batches, 6 teachers, 10 months). QLens reports the keys (\code{student\_name},
\code{month}) and (\code{student\_phone}, \code{month}), six core dependencies, and two inconsistencies:
four students spelled differently on 8 rows, and Mr.~Gupta's old phone number on 5 rows. After
``accept all suggestions'' it proposes \tbl{students}, \tbl{batches}, \tbl{teachers} and
\tbl{fee\_register}; applying the design rebuilds all 3{,}000 rows exactly. A teacher's phone is now
stored once.

\paragraph{The developer migrating legacy data.} \stdone{} A developer uploads an undocumented CSV export
and reads off the keys and dependencies that actually hold, with the share of violating rows next to each
approximate one, which shows where the legacy data cannot be trusted. The proposal comes with generated
\code{CREATE TABLE} statements that can be copied into the application's migrations, or QLens can migrate
the data itself and prove that nothing was lost.

\paragraph{The student learning normalization.} \stdone{} for analysis and design, \stwip{} for querying.
A student opens the hostel mess sample and sees why \code{room} determines \code{hostel\_block}, how the
minimal cover is derived and why each table of the proposal exists, then queries the result. A
clause-by-clause visualizer and a performance laboratory with real execution plans are \stplan.
